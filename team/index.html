What we do

me@aaim.sh ~/team $ cat README.md

# README

We lead delivery squads inside enterprises. One of us joins each squad as its solution architect: writes the design down, builds the risky parts, reviews the rest, and stays until the system is live and someone on your side can run it.

We come from Saudi systems that move money, check identity, or page someone at 3 a.m. when they break: SARIE instant payments, AML screening on Kafka, single sign-on for a government authority, STC's customer-facing APIs, and a hotel platform that takes bookings around the clock.

# house rules: decisions in writing · riskiest path first · no launch without a tested rollback · architects write code

The squad architect role

me@aaim.sh ~/team $ man squad-architect
SQUAD-ARCHITECT(7)aaim.sh/teamSQUAD-ARCHITECT(7)

NAME

squad-architect - the engineer who answers for how a squad's system is designed, built, secured and run

SYNOPSIS

squad-architect --squad <name> [--on-site] [--lang ar|en]

DESCRIPTION

Joins one squad for the length of the program. Sits in its standups, commits to its repo, and is the person your leadership asks whether the system will hold on launch day.

OWNS

  • the design, as architecture decision records in your repo, including the options we turned down and why
  • integration contracts with core systems, vendors and government platforms
  • non-functional requirements, agreed in numbers: p99 latency, peak TPS, availability, RPO/RTO
  • the security review: threat model before build, findings closed before go-live
  • code on the critical path: payments, identity, anything that holds personal data
  • production readiness: dashboards, alerts, runbooks, and a rollback someone has actually run

DOES NOT OWN

  • the backlog and its priorities. That's your product owner.
  • line management of your engineers
  • vendor selection. We write the technical evaluation; you make the call.

WORKS WITH

  • your EA office: we follow your standards, and push back in writing when one blocks delivery
  • your security team: they see the threat model before anything is built
  • your vendors: their designs and code get reviewed against the same bar as ours

EXIT STATUS

0handed over. A named engineer on your side runs it without us. 1still needs us. We count that as unfinished work, not a renewal.

How an engagement runs

me@aaim.sh ~/team $ cat engagement.log
w01–02 read the code, the incident history and recent postmortems. sit with ops.→ current-state map and a risk list, reviewed with your leads. no proposals yet. w03–04 target design for the squad's scope, written as ADRs.→ NFRs agreed in numbers. integration contracts drafted with the teams on the other side. w05+ build with the squad. riskiest integration first, while there's still time to be wrong. release readiness review before every go-live: load test, alerts, runbooks, a rehearsed rollback. handover starts in week one. an engineer from your side pairs with the architect from the first ADR.

Who this is for

me@aaim.sh ~/team $ ./fit --check <your-program>

✓ good fit

  • a program with several squads, a hard date, and systems you can't switch off to deploy
  • integrations with payment rails, government platforms, or a core system older than some of your engineers
  • you have engineers and product owners, and you're missing the person who owns the design and answers for it in production

✗ not a fit

  • architecture for a tender that nobody plans to build
  • headcount without decision rights. An architect who can't decide is a note-taker.
  • fixed-scope delivery handed to a vendor end to end. That's a systems integrator's job.
