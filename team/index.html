Here's what we actually do

Your architect joins one squad for the whole program. Same standups, same repo, same deadline.

  1. Write the design down.Every decision goes in your repo as an ADR, with the options we turned down and why.
  2. Pin the numbers.p99 latency, peak TPS, availability, RPO/RTO. Agreed with the business before anyone builds.
  3. Own the integrations.Contracts with your core systems, vendors and government platforms, worked out with the team on the other side.
  4. Build the scary parts.Payments, identity, anything holding personal data. We write that code ourselves.
  5. Get it through security.Your security team sees the threat model before we build, and findings are closed before go-live.
  6. Make it boring to run.Dashboards, alerts, runbooks, and a rollback someone has actually tried.

Stuff we don't do

  • Run your backlog. That's your product owner's job.
  • Manage your people.
  • Pick your vendors. We write the technical evaluation, you make the call.
  • Go around your EA office. We follow your standards and push back in writing when one gets in the way.

How it goes

  1. Weeks 1–2

    Listen first

    We read the code, the incident history and your recent postmortems, and sit with ops. You get a map of what exists and a list of risks. No proposals yet.

  2. Weeks 3–4

    Decide on paper

    Target design as ADRs, NFRs in numbers, and integration contracts drafted with the teams on the other side.

  3. Week 5 on

    Build

    Riskiest integration first, while there's still time to be wrong.

  4. Every release

    Prove it

    Load test, alerts, runbooks and a rehearsed rollback before anything goes live.

  5. From day one

    Hand it over

    An engineer from your side pairs with the architect from the first ADR, so there's no big handover at the end.

What stays in your repo

  • ADRs
  • C4 diagrams
  • NFR sheet
  • Threat model
  • OpenAPI / AsyncAPI contracts
  • Runbooks

Nothing important lives in a slide deck or in our heads. If the system still needs us after handover, we count that as unfinished work, not a renewal.
