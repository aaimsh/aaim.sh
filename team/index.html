What the architect does

Each architect is assigned to one squad for the full program and works within the squad's existing routines.

  1. RequirementsRuns workshops with business owners and documents requirements ready for the product owner's backlog.
  2. Architecture decisionsRecords each design decision as an architecture decision record (ADR), with the alternatives considered.
  3. Non-functional targetsAgrees measurable targets with the business before build: latency, throughput, availability, RPO and RTO.
  4. IntegrationsDefines contracts with core systems, vendors and government platforms together with the teams that own them.
  5. SecurityShares the threat model with your security team before build and tracks findings to closure before go-live.
  6. Release readinessApproves go-live once dashboards, alerts, runbooks and a tested rollback are in place.

Outside our scope

  • Writing code. Your engineers build the system.
  • Backlog priorities. These stay with your product owner.
  • People management.
  • Vendor selection. We provide the technical evaluation; the decision is yours.
  • Bypassing your architecture standards. We follow them and raise exceptions in writing.

How an engagement runs

  1. Weeks 1–2

    Assessment

    Review of the current architecture, incident history and recent postmortems. Output: a current-state map and a risk register.

  2. Weeks 3–4

    Target design

    Requirements, architecture decisions, agreed non-functional targets and draft integration contracts, reviewed with your leads.

  3. Week 5 onward

    Delivery

    The squad builds against the agreed design, starting with the highest-risk integration.

  4. Each release

    Readiness review

    Load test results, alerts, runbooks and a rehearsed rollback are checked before go-live.

  5. Throughout

    Handover

    An engineer from your team works with the architect from the start, so knowledge transfer happens during delivery.

Deliverables

  • Requirements
  • Architecture decision records
  • C4 diagrams
  • Non-functional targets
  • Threat model

When to engage us

Good fit

  • Programs with several squads and a fixed delivery date
  • Integrations with payment rails, government platforms or legacy core systems
  • Teams with engineers and product owners but no single owner of the overall design

Not a fit

  • Architecture documents for a tender with no delivery plan
  • Staff augmentation without design authority
  • Fixed-scope, end-to-end delivery, which a systems integrator is better placed to handle
